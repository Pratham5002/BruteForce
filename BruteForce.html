    📚 Stay safe online — everything you need to know

    Simple, jargon-free lessons about how hackers really work and how to stop them. No prior tech knowledge needed.

    Why this matters

    Most people think hacking looks like a movie — a hooded figure typing furiously. In reality, 81% of breaches start with a weak or stolen password. Hackers don't break in — they log in with credentials they bought, guessed, or tricked you into giving them. The good news: a few simple habits block almost all of them.

    01

    How hackers actually attack

    Six common methods — and how each one is stopped

    🔨Brute Force
    A computer tries every possible combination of characters until it finds yours. Simple passwords fall in seconds.
    GPU rig tries 10,000,000,000 guesses/sec
    Defence: Use 12+ characters. Every extra character multiplies the time by ~100×.
    📖Dictionary Attack
    Instead of random guessing, the attacker tries common words, names, and known passwords from leak lists — millions per second.
    Tries "password", "dragon", "prathmesh123" first
    Defence: Never use a single dictionary word. Combine 3–4 random words instead.
    🎣Phishing
    A fake email, SMS, or website tricks you into typing your password on a page that looks real but isn't.
    "Your Amazon account is locked — click here to verify"
    Defence: Never click links in emails. Open the app or type the site address yourself.
    🔄Credential Stuffing
    If one site you use gets breached, attackers try that same email + password combo on every other site — Gmail, bank, Amazon, everything.
    One leak = 100 accounts compromised
    Defence: Use a different password for every single site. A password manager makes this painless.
    🎭Social Engineering
    A human tricks you into revealing a password — pretending to be IT support, a bank agent, or a friend in trouble.
    "Hi, I'm from your bank — please confirm your OTP"
    Defence: Legitimate companies never ask for your password or OTP. Hang up and call back on the official number.
    🕵️Shoulder Surfing / Spyware
    Someone watches you type, or malware on your device records your keystrokes and screenshots. Public Wi-Fi and cracked apps are common sources.
    Keylogger records every keystroke silently
    Defence: Install apps only from official stores. Use a screen privacy filter in public. Keep your OS updated.
    02

    Password do's & don'ts

    The 10 rules that matter most

    ✅ DO this

    • ✓Use 12+ characters — length beats complexity.
    • ✓Use a different password on every site.
    • ✓Use a password manager (Bitwarden, 1Password, KeePass).
    • ✓Turn on 2FA everywhere it's offered.
    • ✓Use a passphrase like blue-cactus-river-91!
    • ✓Change passwords immediately after a breach alert.
    • ✓Check your email on haveibeenpwned.com occasionally.
    • ✓Keep your recovery email up to date.

    ❌ DON'T do this

    • ✗Don't use names, birthdays, or cities.
    • ✗Don't reuse the same password on multiple sites.
    • ✗Don't use "password123", "qwerty", "admin" — ever.
    • ✗Don't share passwords over WhatsApp, email, or SMS.
    • ✗Don't write passwords in a notepad or sticky note.
    • ✗Don't click "remember me" on shared computers.
    • ✗Don't ignore breach notifications from Google or Apple.
    • ✗Don't type passwords on public Wi-Fi without HTTPS.
    03

    Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)

    Your single best upgrade — 99% of automated attacks blocked

    What it is: A second proof of identity on top of your password. Even if a hacker steals your password, they can't log in without the second factor — which only you have.

    1
    You enter your password
    Something you know — your password or PIN.
    2
    The site asks for a second factor
    Something you have (phone, security key) or something you are (fingerprint, face).
    3
    You approve the login
    The hacker can't — they don't have your phone or fingerprint.

    Types of 2FA — best to worst

    🔑
    Hardware key / Passkey
    Most secure. A physical USB key or biometric passkey. Nearly impossible to phish.
    📱
    Authenticator app
    Google Authenticator, Authy, Microsoft Authenticator. Works offline. Very secure.
    💬
    SMS / Email OTP
    Better than nothing, but can be intercepted via SIM-swap. Use only if nothing else exists.
    💡 Turn on 2FA today for: Gmail, bank apps, UPI (Google Pay/PhonePe/Paytm), Instagram, WhatsApp, Amazon, Facebook, and any account that has your money or identity.
    04

    How to spot a phishing attack

    Eight warning signs — if you see two or more, it's a scam

    ⚠️
    Urgency or threats
    "Your account will be closed in 24 hours" — real companies don't do this.
    📧
    Wrong sender address
    "support@amaz0n-help.xyz" instead of "@amazon.com". Always check the domain carefully.
    🔗
    Suspicious link
    Hover before clicking. If the domain isn't the official one, don't click.
    📝
    Spelling & grammar mistakes
    "Dear Valued Custmer, your acount has been susspended." Real brands proofread.
    🙋
    Asks for personal info
    Legitimate companies never ask for your password, OTP, or CVV by email.
    💰
    Too-good-to-be-true offers
    "You've won ₹50,000! Claim now." If it's unexpected, it's fake.
    📎
    Unexpected attachments
    .zip, .exe, .docm files from unknown senders often carry malware.
    📞
    Unusual contact method
    Your bank doesn't WhatsApp you asking for details. Neither does the tax office.

    🛡️ The 3-step phishing defence

    1. Stop. Don't click, don't reply, don't call the number in the message.
    2. Verify. Open the official app or type the website address manually to check your account.
    3. Report. Forward the email to reportphishing@apwg.org or use the "report spam" button.
    05

    What to do if you've been hacked

    Act fast — the first 24 hours matter most

    1
    Change your password immediately
    Start with your email account — that's the master key to everything else. Then change the password on the compromised site.
    2
    Change it everywhere you reused it
    If you used the same password on other sites, change them all now. This is why unique passwords matter.
    3
    Turn on 2FA if you haven't
    Even if the attacker has your new password, 2FA will lock them out. Do it on email, banking, and social media first.
    4
    Check for unauthorised activity
    Look at your bank statements, sent emails, and account login history. Report anything suspicious to the service.
    5
    Scan your devices for malware
    Run a full antivirus scan. A keylogger may still be sitting on your computer or phone recording everything.
    6
    Tell your contacts
    If your email or WhatsApp was hacked, warn friends — they may receive scam messages pretending to be you.
    7
    Report it
    In India: cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930 (national cybercrime helpline). Also notify your bank if money is involved.
